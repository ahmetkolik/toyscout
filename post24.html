What to look for

  • Size. A hand puppet has to fit the child’s hand. The Bluey puppets are 8 to 9 inches, made to fit a toddler. The Melissa & Doug sets say they fit kids and most adults, so a parent can join the show.
  • Soft plush or hard rubber. Plush puppets are for cuddling and storytelling. The rubber shark is a play toy, not a cuddly one.
  • One puppet or a set. A set gives you enough characters for a show with a story, and one character puppet suits a child who has a favorite.
  • Washable. The Melissa & Doug and GUND listings say surface-washable. Check the care line on the listing for any puppet that will go in the car or the bath.
  • Finger puppets for water and travel. The Baby Shark and Mickey sets are plastic and slip onto fingers. Their listings pitch them for bath time, party favors and stocking stuffers.

What to skip

  • A puppet that is too big for the hand. If a child cannot reach the mouth, the puppet will not talk and the show will not happen.
  • Rubber puppets for the youngest. The rubber shark has sharp-looking teeth and is a play toy, not a cuddly one. Check the age line on the listing.
  • A craft kit for a toddler. The glue, pompoms and googly eyes are small parts, so it is for older kids with an adult at the table.

Frequently asked questions

What age are hand puppets good for?

From about 2. The Melissa & Doug Safari set is sold for ages 2 and up and the GUND Cookie Monster for ages 1 and up.

Hand puppets or finger puppets?

Hand puppets are better for storytelling and cuddling. Finger puppets are small, easy to pack and good for bath time and for songs.

Do puppets help shy kids?

The Melissa & Doug listings point to language development and confidence as benefits of puppet play. We have not tested this ourselves.
