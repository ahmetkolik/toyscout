Magna-Tiles vs PicassoTiles at a glance

  • Pieces. The Magna-Tiles Classic set has 32 pieces, 14 small squares, 2 large squares and 16 triangles, in 6 translucent colors. The PicassoTiles set has 100 pieces, so you can build much bigger from day one.
  • Ratings. Magna-Tiles Classic is rated 4.9★ and PicassoTiles 4.8★. Both are strong, and the PicassoTiles set has about three times as many ratings.
  • Brand. Magna-Tiles calls itself the original magnetic building brand, and its listing says the Classic set is a certified STEAM toy per Dr. Gummer’s Good Play Guide, in partnership with The Toy Association. That is the listing’s claim, not our test.
  • Which to buy. If you want the brand and the simplest first set, Magna-Tiles Classic. If you want the most pieces in one box, PicassoTiles. Check the price of each on Amazon, since it changes often.

What to look for

  • Piece count and shapes. Squares and triangles are the base. A few dozen pieces make houses and towers, and a hundred let a child build big castles and rooms. More pieces are the best upgrade.
  • Translucent or solid. Classic Magna-Tiles are translucent, so light shines through. Printed or themed sets add roads, animals or decorations to a base of ordinary shapes.
  • Compatibility. Several listings say their tiles work with other brands. We cannot confirm it, so if you plan to mix sets, read the compatibility line and the reviews first.
  • Storage. Magnetic tiles stick together, so a bin or a tin keeps them tidy. The microMAGS tin doubles as a baseplate.
  • Safety. Magnets are a hazard if they come loose. Look for sealed magnets, which some listings mention, and keep sets away from children who still put things in their mouths.

What to skip

  • Mixing brands without checking. Compatibility claims are the sellers’. Test a small set before you buy a large one.
  • Magnetic sets for children who still mouth things. Use sets sold for ages 3 and up, and check for loose magnets.
  • Choosing by piece count alone. A 100-piece set of the wrong shapes can be worse than 32 well-chosen ones.

Frequently asked questions

Are PicassoTiles the same as Magna-Tiles?

No, they are different brands. Magna-Tiles describes itself as the original magnetic building brand. Both make translucent magnetic tiles in squares and triangles.

Do Magna-Tiles and PicassoTiles work together?

We have not tested it. Some listings claim compatibility with other brands, so check the listing and the reviews.

What age are magnetic tiles for?

The listings here are sold for ages 3 and up. Younger children who put things in their mouths should not use them.

How many magnetic tiles do you need?

A 32-piece set is enough for houses and towers. A 100-piece set lets a child build much larger structures.
